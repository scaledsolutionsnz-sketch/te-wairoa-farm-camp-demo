What you get

Staying at Te Wairoa

No numbered bays, no booking portal, no power hookups. A paddock above the beach, a portaloo, an outdoor shower and about as much room as you want.

Tent sites on the dune flat

Pick your own spot on the grass above the beach. Nothing is marked out, so spread out and take the view you want.

Motorhomes, vans and caravans

Unpowered sites with room to tow in and turn around. Non self contained vehicles are welcome here, which rules a lot of Northland out.

Dogs are welcome

Bring the dog. Keep them under control around the stock and they get the run of the best beach walk in the Far North.

Surfcasting and the beach

The southern end of Ninety Mile Beach starts just past the dunes. Walk down for a cast, a swim, or a look at what the tide brought in.

Sunsets over the Tasman

The west coast sunset lands right in front of your spot. Bring a chair and something warm, it gets fresh once the sun drops.

A working beef farm

The rest of the year this is a beef farm. Over summer the beach frontage opens up, so you camp on a paddock rather than a car park.

About the camp

A beef farm that opens its gate to campers over summer

Te Wairoa sits at the southern end of Ninety Mile Beach, out along Sandhills Road from Ahipara. For most of the year the beach frontage is farmland. Over the summer months it comes out of the farm rotation and opens up for tents, vans and caravans.

The facilities are basic on purpose. Portaloo toilets, outdoor showers, an outdoor kitchen and rubbish bins. If you are after flush toilets and hot water, there are holiday parks back in town. If you want old school camping with the surf in front of you, this is the one.

  • Ten dollars a person a night, sorted on site
  • Beach frontage, not a back paddock down the road
  • Non self contained vehicles and dogs are both welcome
